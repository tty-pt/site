```
                                               │                            │

                    (Compiles for nd.so)       │                            │ (Compiles for nd.wasm)

                      │    - Zero GPU/GL dependencies  │           │     - 55° Tilted Camera Matrix │
                      │    - Instant SSR DOM shell     │           │     - GPU Z-Buffer & Cutouts   │
                      └────────────────────────────────┘           └────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Invariants

1. **Server (`mods/nd/nd.so`)**: Remains **100% pure software** in C. Zero WebGL, zero GPU dependencies, zero display server assumptions. It renders the fallback 2D frame into `g_lamina_fb` using CPU rasterization for headless Server-Side Rendering (SSR).
2. **Client (`htdocs/nd.wasm` + WebGL)**: Runs in the browser via WebAssembly and takes **full advantage of client hardware acceleration**. It evaluates the 3D camera projection, elevates the terrain mesh to $Z = H(wx, wy)$, tests against the hardware depth buffer, and blits upright billboard sprites at their true $(x, y, z)$ elevations.
3. **Zero `#ifdef` Compile-Time Branching in UX Code**: The C code in `mods/nd/ux/` is 100% isomorphic. Target-specific dispatch is encapsulated at the systems ABI boundary in `<ttypt/lam.h>`.
4. **No Site-Specific JavaScript**: `htdocs/lamina-bridge.js` contains zero NeverDark-specific identifiers, URLs, or game logic, remaining strictly generic graphics infrastructure in compliance with `scripts/check-no-site-specific-js.sh`.

## 2. Coordinate Systems & The Subtle-Tilt Camera Geometry

### World Space Coordinates
- **$X$ axis**: West $\rightarrow$ East (increases horizontally to the right).
- **$Y$ axis**: North $\rightarrow$ South (increases downwards in room index; 1 room = $160 \times 128$ continuous world units).
- **$Z$ axis**: Nadir $\rightarrow$ Zenith (elevation above sea level; increases vertically upward into the sky).

### Orbital Camera Parameters
The camera centers on the player/lamb target point $\vec{T} = (T_x, T_y, T_z)$ where $T_z = H(T_x, T_y)$:
- **Pitch Angle ($\theta$)**: $55^\circ$ from horizontal (tilted down towards North).
- **Yaw Angle ($\phi$)**: $0^\circ$ (aligned facing North, along $-Y$).
- **Distance ($D$)**: $18.0$ world units (calibrated so visible screen width matches the 7×7 NeverDark room window).
- **Vertical Field of View ($\alpha$)**: $45^\circ$ perspective.

### Vector Mathematics & Orthonormal Frame
$$\vec{Forward} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -\cos(55^\circ) \\ -\sin(55^\circ) \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -0.5736 \\ -0.8192 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\vec{Up} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -\sin(55^\circ) \\ \cos(55^\circ) \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -0.8192 \\ +0.5736 \end{pmatrix}$$

$$\vec{Right} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \quad (\text{East})$$

This forms an exact right-handed orthonormal camera frame:
$$\vec{Right} \times \vec{Up} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \times \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -\sin(55^\circ) \\ \cos(55^\circ) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -\cos(55^\circ) \\ -\sin(55^\circ) \end{pmatrix} = \vec{Forward}$$

### Camera Eye Position in World Space
$$\vec{E} = \begin{pmatrix} T_x \\ T_y + D \cdot \cos(55^\circ) \\ T_z + D \cdot \sin(55^\circ) \end{pmatrix}$$

The camera sits **South of the lamb** ($+Y$) and **elevated in the sky** ($+Z$), looking down and North towards the lamb.

### The Projection Formula & Elevation Physics
For any world point $\vec{P} = (x, y, z)$ on the terrain:
- Offset relative to camera eye: $\vec{\Delta} = \vec{P} - \vec{E}$.
- Camera-space coordinates:
  $$X_{cam} = \vec{\Delta} \cdot \vec{Right} = x - E_x$$
  $$Y_{cam} = \vec{\Delta} \cdot \vec{Up} = \Delta_y \cdot (-\sin\theta) + \Delta_z \cdot (\cos\theta)$$
  $$Z_{cam} = -\vec{\Delta} \cdot \vec{Forward} = \Delta_y \cdot (\cos\theta) + \Delta_z \cdot (\sin\theta)$$

Projected onto normalized device coordinates:
$$x_{ndc} = \frac{X_{cam}}{Z_{cam} \cdot \tan(\alpha/2) \cdot aspect}$$
$$y_{ndc} = \frac{Y_{cam}}{Z_{cam} \cdot \tan(\alpha/2)}$$

#### Why This Creates True 3D Hills:
In $Y_{cam}$, notice that higher elevation ($z > 0$) contributes $+\Delta_z \cdot \cos(55^\circ) > 0$.
**A hill rising in $+Z$ pushes the projected vertex physically upward on screen**, exactly matching the visual reality of viewing elevated terrain from an aerial tilted perspective!

### Horizon Frustum Culling Margin
In 2D top-down mode, `nd_seven.c` calculates chunk visibility bounds $[j_0..j_1]$ using a simple rectangular bounding box. Because the $55^\circ$ tilted perspective camera looks further North into the distance:
- The northern visible chunk row boundary $j_0$ is expanded by 2 chunk rows ($j_0 \leftarrow j_0 - 2$).
- This guarantees that distant hills and mountain ridges recede smoothly toward the upper horizon without pop-in at the canvas boundary.

## 3. Continuous 3D Terrain Mesh Pipeline (Client Hardware)

### A. Data Source
In `nd_seven_ground.c`, `chunk_get_or_create()` evaluates continuous planetary elevation and water tables:
```c
nd_field_sample_t fs = nd_field_sample(world, 3, wx, wy);
chunk->height[idx] = fs.elevation; // int16_t elevation in world units
chunk->pixels[idx] = pixel_col;    // 32-bit RGBA biome substrate + diffuse sun hillshading
chunk->depth[idx]  = fs.depth;     // Water depth = W - H
```

### B. GPU Geometry Representation
Instead of uploading a flat 2D rectangle to the canvas, the WebGL pipeline constructs an indexed grid mesh:
- **Grid Resolution**: $160 \times 128$ quads per chunk (matching continuous cell resolution).
- **Static Vertex Grid VBO**: Pre-allocated once at startup with normalized $(u, v) \in [0..1] \times [0..1]$ coordinates and static triangle strip index buffers.

### C. Exact 16-Bit Integer Elevation Texture (`R16I`)
In `chunk->height`, NeverDark stores elevation as signed `int16_t` (negative for underwater ocean floors, positive for hills).
- Under WebGL2, `chunk->height` is uploaded directly to the GPU as an `R16I` integer texture:
  ```javascript
  gl.texImage2D(gl.TEXTURE_2D, 0, gl.R16I, width, height, 0, gl.RED_INTEGER, gl.SHORT, heightArray);
  ```
- In the vertex shader, reading `uniform isampler2D uHeightTex` extracts the exact signed elevation without any 8-bit quantization or precision loss:
  ```glsl
  float wz = float(texture(uHeightTex, a_uv).r);
  ```

### D. 3D Terrain Shaders

#### Vertex Shader (`VS_TERRAIN_3D`)
```glsl
#version 300 es
layout (location = 0) in vec2 a_uv; // [0..1, 0..1]

uniform mat4 uMVP;
uniform isampler2D uHeightTex;    // R16I exact elevation texture
uniform vec2 uChunkWorldOrigin;  // (ox, oy) in world units
uniform vec2 uChunkWorldSpan;    // (160.0, 128.0)
uniform float uHeightScale;      // Elevation scale factor (default 1.0)

out vec2 vUV;
out float vZ;

void main() {
    vUV = a_uv;
    
    // Exact signed 16-bit elevation from continuous field
    int raw_h = texture(uHeightTex, a_uv).r;
    float wz = float(raw_h) * uHeightScale;
    vZ = wz;

    vec3 worldPos = vec3(
        uChunkWorldOrigin.x + a_uv.x * uChunkWorldSpan.x,
        uChunkWorldOrigin.y + a_uv.y * uChunkWorldSpan.y,
        wz
    );

    gl_Position = uMVP * vec4(worldPos, 1.0);
}
```

#### Fragment Shader (`FS_TERRAIN_3D`)
```glsl
#version 300 es
precision mediump float;
in vec2 vUV;
in float vZ;

uniform sampler2D uSubstrateTex; // chunk->pixels (biomes + diffuse sun hillshading)
out vec4 FragColor;

void main() {
    FragColor = texture(uSubstrateTex, vUV);
}
```

### E. Translucent 3D Water Plane
- The water plane is a geometric horizontal quad situated at global sea level:
  $$Z_{\text{water}} = W(wx, wy)$$
- From the $55^\circ$ tilted angle, rays from the camera pass through the water plane at $Z = W$ before striking the seabed at $Z = H < W$:
  - Water Depth: $d = W - H$.
  - Beer–Lambert Absorption: $T(d) = e^{-\alpha \cdot d}$.
  - Shallow shallows ($d \le 4$ units): High transparency ($>80\%$), allowing golden sand, submerged pebbles, and swimming fish to be seen clearly.
  - Deep ocean ($d > 20$ units): Dark navy/turquoise opacity, with animated wave caustics and highlights moving across the top surface.

## 4. Multi-Elevation Billboard Pipeline (Entities with $Z$)

Every dynamic object in NeverDark possesses a 3D coordinate $(wx, wy, Z)$ and an upright billboard geometry:

```
          [Top-Left]                  [Top-Right]
           (x - 0.5W, y, Z + H)        (x + 0.5W, y, Z + H)
                     ┌──────────────────┐
                     │                  │
                     │   Sprite Body    │
                     │   (Tree / Mob)   │
                     │                  │
                     └────────┬─────────┘
           (x - 0.5W, y, Z)   │        (x + 0.5W, y, Z)
          [Bot-Left]          ▼        [Bot-Right]
                         [Foot Contact]
                          (wx, wy, Z)
```

### Billboard Vertex Construction
Because living entities, trees, and rocks stand vertically along world $+Z$, their quads are generated on the GPU using:
$$\vec{Right}_{\text{cam}} = (1, 0, 0)$$
$$\vec{Up}_{\text{world}} = (0, 0, 1)$$

Given world position $(x, y, Z)$, sprite width $W_s$, height $H_s$, and anchor $(a_x, a_y)$ (default $(0.5, 0.0)$ for feet):
- **Corner 0 (Bottom-Left)**: $\begin{pmatrix} x - a_x W_s \\ y \\ Z - a_y H_s \end{pmatrix}$
- **Corner 1 (Bottom-Right)**: $\begin{pmatrix} x + (1 - a_x) W_s \\ y \\ Z - a_y H_s \end{pmatrix}$
- **Corner 2 (Top-Right)**: $\begin{pmatrix} x + (1 - a_x) W_s \\ y \\ Z + (1 - a_y) H_s \end{pmatrix}$
- **Corner 3 (Top-Left)**: $\begin{pmatrix} x - a_x W_s \\ y \\ Z + (1 - a_y) H_s \end{pmatrix}$

### Entity Classification across Z-Levels

| Entity Category | Elevation Model | Visual Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Lamb & Players** | $Z = H(wx, wy)$ | Feet ride the continuous elevation curve. As the lamb walks up a dune or hill, its foot position rises along the terrain slope. |
| **Surface Flora & Rocks** | $Z = H(wx, wy)$ | Anchored directly to the ground slope. Large boulders and oak trees extend into the air, occluding characters walking behind them via the Z-buffer. |
| **Birds & Flying Mobs** | $Z = H(wx, wy) + \Delta z_{\text{alt}}$ | Fly through the air above trees. A circular shadow quad is rendered on the terrain at $(wx, wy, H(wx, wy))$ so altitude is visually grounded. |
| **Fish & Underwater Mobs** | $H(wx, wy) \le Z < W$ | Swim below sea level $W$, visible through the translucent water surface. |
| **Subterranean Cavities** | $Z < H(wx, wy)$ | Digging and cave exploration beneath the surface (scheduled for follow-up milestone). |

### One-Time GPU Sprite Atlas Upload
`nd_seven_atlas_draw.c` decompresses the sprite atlas (`s_atlas`) into memory once on startup via RLE.
- In WebGL, this atlas is uploaded once to the GPU as `uAtlasTex`.
- `lam_billboard_batch_flush` passes compact instance buffers containing sprite IDs, which index $(u_0, v_0, u_1, v_1)$ directly from `nd_seven_sprite_rects[sprite_id]`.

### Alpha Cutout (Crucial for Z-Buffer Correctness)
2D sprite textures have transparent borders. In 3D, transparent pixels must **never write to the depth buffer**, or they create invisible occlusion boxes. The fragment shader discards transparent pixels:
```glsl
vec4 col = texture(uAtlasTex, vUV);
if (col.a < 0.1) discard; // Leaves depth buffer untouched for background terrain
FragColor = col;
```

## 5. Input & Ray-Plane Intersection (Picking on 3D Terrain)

When the player clicks on the canvas at screen pixel $(s_x, s_y)$:
1. Transform $(s_x, s_y)$ to Normalized Device Coordinates:
   $$x_{ndc} = \frac{2 s_x}{W_{canvas}} - 1.0, \quad y_{ndc} = 1.0 - \frac{2 s_y}{H_{canvas}}$$
2. Unproject near and far clipping points using $(uMVP)^{-1}$:
   $$\vec{P}_{near} = (uMVP)^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} x_{ndc} \\ y_{ndc} \\ -1.0 \\ 1.0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \vec{P}_{far} = (uMVP)^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} x_{ndc} \\ y_{ndc} \\ 1.0 \\ 1.0 \end{pmatrix}$$
3. Form the picking ray: $\vec{R}(t) = \vec{P}_0 + t \cdot \vec{D}$ with $\vec{D} = \text{normalize}(\vec{P}_{far} - \vec{P}_{near})$.
4. Solve intersection with the local continuous terrain surface $Z = H(x, y)$ using a 4-step secant iteration starting from the mean ground plane. This yields the exact world coordinate $(wx, wy)$ under the cursor for tile harvesting, item interaction, and pathfinding.

 1. Ground Truth & Root Cause Analysis

 ### What Has Actually Been Drawing On Screen

 - The screen has been 100% 2D orthographic pixel art:
   In mods/nd/ux/nd_ui.c (lines 791–799), lam_canvas_flush(g_lamina_fb, ...) is called every frame immediately
   following lam_terrain_mesh_flush(...).
   lam_canvas_flush takes the 2D CPU software framebuffer g_lamina_fb (rasterized by nd_seven_render) and uploads it
   as a flat texture onto a full-screen 2D quad, completely painting over whatever the 3D pipeline drew.
 - Zero 3D terrain triangles have ever been visibly seen:
   Whenever lam_canvas_flush was commented out to test the 3D pipeline, the screen turned solid pitch-black.
 - Why the screen went black instead of showing 3D:
     1. lam_terrain_mesh_flush calls gl.clearColor(0.04, 0.04, 0.05, 1.0); gl.clear(...).
     2. The orbital camera distance was set to 18.0f (with FOV 45^∘), while a single room in NeverDark is 160 × 128
        units wide. The camera was sitting directly inside or behind the terrain elevation, causing WebGL near-plane
        clipping (z_near = 0.5) to cull 100% of the triangles.
     3. The texture bindings for R16I integer elevation (uHeightTex) and 32-bit substrate (uSubstrateTex) had origin
        and dimension mismatches.
 - Status of Lighting:
   Dead in practice. While GLSL lighting math (N · L) was added to VS_TERRAIN_3D in lamina-bridge.js, it never
   reached the screen because the 3D mesh was either clipped or covered by the 2D quad.
 - Status of Billboard Z-Scaling & Parallax:
   Non-existent. All entities in nd_seven.c are drawn with a fixed constant SCALE = 2 without perspective projection
   or depth offsets.

 3. Subquest 1: Real 3D Terrain Triangles with Lighting (Replace 2D Ground)

 ### Objective

 Render the physical continuous terrain using real WebGL 3D triangle vertices displaced by 16-bit elevation (Z =
 H(wx, wy)) and illuminated by directional sunlight (N · L). Replace the 2D software ground quad with this 3D
 pipeline, governed by a compile-time constant toggle.

 ### Architectural Invariants & Rules

 1. Constant Toggle:
    In mods/nd/ux/nd_ui.c:
    ```c
      /* 0 = Legacy 2D software CPU blit; 1 = True 3D WebGL terrain mesh */
      #define ND_SEVEN_3D_TERRAIN 1
    ```
     - When ND_SEVEN_3D_TERRAIN == 0: lam_canvas_flush runs as before, producing the legacy 2D view.
     - When ND_SEVEN_3D_TERRAIN == 1: lam_canvas_flush does not draw the ground over the 3D terrain;
       lam_terrain_mesh_flush renders the actual 3D triangle mesh directly onto #seven-stage-canvas.
 2. Server Headless SSR:
    On the native server (nd.so), <ttypt/lam.h> inlines empty stubs for lam_terrain_mesh_flush. The server continues
    pure CPU rasterization into g_lamina_fb. All native unit tests (tests/unit/run-test-seven-render.sh) must pass
    100%.
 3. Lighting Specification:
     - Surface Normals (N⃗): Computed in vertex/fragment shader by sampling neighboring heights (h_R - h_L, h_U -
       h_D):
       vec(dX) = ⎛ 2 · spanₓ · Δ u ⎞, vec(dY) = ⎛ 0                ⎞, N⃗ = normalize(vec(dX) × vec(dY))
                 ⎜ 0               ⎟            ⎜ 2 · span_y · Δ v ⎟
                 ⎝ h_R - h_L       ⎠            ⎝ h_U - h_D        ⎠
     - Directional Sunlight Vector (L⃗): Slanted from Northwest down toward the terrain:
       L⃗ = normalize⎛ -0.577 ⎞
                    ⎜ 0.577  ⎟
                    ⎝ 0.816  ⎠
     - Illumination Factor:
       Light = I_ambient + I_diffuse · max (0, N⃗ · L⃗)
       with I_ambient = 0.45 and I_diffuse = 0.55. Sunward slopes will be brightly illuminated; opposite slopes and
       depressions will be shaded.

 4. Subquest 2: Perspective Billboard Z-Scaling & Vertical Mob Elevation

 ### Objective

 Position all dynamic entities (the lamb, NPCs, flora, boulders) at their authentic world height Z = H(wx, wy) using
 nd_seven_ground_elevation(). Render them as upright 3D billboards facing the camera with perspective distance
 scaling (1/Z).

 ### Architectural Specification (from 25D.md §4)

 1. Vertical Foot Anchoring:
    For any entity at world position (wx, wy):
    wz = H(wx, wy) = nd_seven_ground_elevation(wx, wy)
    The foot anchor (0.5, 0.0) is placed exactly on the ground.
 2. Upright Billboard Vertices:
    Billboards stand along world vec(Up) = (0, 0, 1) and face camera vec(Right) = (1, 0, 0):
     - Bottom-Left: (wx - aₓ Wₛ, wy, wz)
     - Bottom-Right: (wx + (1 - aₓ) Wₛ, wy, wz)
     - Top-Right: (wx + (1 - aₓ) Wₛ, wy, wz + Hₛ)
     - Top-Left: (wx - aₓ Wₛ, wy, wz + Hₛ)
 3. Perspective Z-Scaling:
    Through the perspective matrix Proj × View, distant objects (larger depth from camera) naturally project to
    smaller screen footprints (1 / Z_cam).
 4. Sprite Atlas Upload & Alpha Cutout:
    The decompressed sprite atlas is uploaded once to WebGL as uAtlasTex. In FS_BILLBOARD_3D:
    ```glsl
      vec4 col = texture(uAtlasTex, vUV) * vTint;
      if (col.a < 0.1) discard;
      FragColor = col;
    ```
    Transparent pixels do not write to the depth buffer, ensuring proper occlusion without transparent box artifacts.
